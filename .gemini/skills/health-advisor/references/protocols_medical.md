# Nuclear & Radiological Emergency Medical Protocols

## 1. Immediate Triage & Decontamination
- **Triage**: Priority is life-threatening injuries (trauma) first. Radiation injury is secondary unless it's a massive dose.
- **Decontamination**:
    - Remove outer clothing: Removes ~90% of external contamination.
    - Wash skin with lukewarm water and soap. Do not scrub (prevents internalizing particles through skin abrasions).
    - Cover wounds with waterproof dressings after cleaning.

## 2. Acute Radiation Syndrome (ARS)
ARS occurs with whole-body doses >1 Gy (100 rads).

### Phases:
1.  **Prodromal**: Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea (NVD) within minutes/hours.
2.  **Latent**: Symptoms subside for days/weeks.
3.  **Manifest Illness**: Hematopoietic (bone marrow), Gastrointestinal (GI), or Cardiovascular (CV)/Central Nervous System (CNS) syndromes.

### Severity Indicators:
- **Time to Vomiting**:
    - < 1 hour: Very high dose (>6 Gy), likely fatal without intensive care.
    - 1-2 hours: High dose (4-6 Gy).
    - > 2 hours: Moderate dose (2-4 Gy).

## 3. Medical Countermeasures
- **Potassium Iodide (KI)**: Blocks radioactive iodine uptake by the thyroid. Take ONLY if instructed by authorities.
- **Prussian Blue**: Enhances excretion of radioactive cesium and thallium.
- **DTPA (Zinc or Calcium)**: Used for internal contamination with plutonium, americium, or curium (chelating agents).
- **Cytokines (e.g., Neupogen)**: Stimulates white blood cell production for hematopoietic syndrome.

## 4. Cutaneous Radiation Injury (CRI)
- Radiation burns may not appear for hours or days.
- Treat like thermal burns: clean, prevent infection, manage pain.
