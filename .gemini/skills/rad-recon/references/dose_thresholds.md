# Radiological Dose Thresholds

Reference guide for understanding radiation levels (nSv/h and mSv).

## 1. Background Levels (Ambient Gamma)
*   **50 - 150 nSv/h**: Normal background levels in France.
*   **150 - 300 nSv/h**: Elevated background (common in granitic regions like Brittany).
*   **> 500 nSv/h**: Significant elevation. Investigation required.

## 2. Acute Exposure (Dose)
*   **< 100 mSv**: No immediate symptoms. Low long-term risk increase.
*   **100 - 500 mSv**: Potential temporary drop in white blood cell count.
*   **1,000 - 2,000 mSv (1-2 Gy)**: Radiation sickness (nausea, vomiting). Recovery likely with medical care.
*   **3,000 - 5,000 mSv (3-5 Gy)**: Severe radiation sickness. 50% fatality without intensive care (LD 50/60).
*   **> 8,000 mSv (> 8 Gy)**: Fatal without immediate, advanced medical intervention.

## 3. Protective Action Guidelines
*   **Sheltering**: Advised if dose rate exceeds **10,000 nSv/h (10 µSv/h)** for sustained periods.
*   **Evacuation**: Mandatory if projected cumulative dose exceeds **50 mSv** over 1 week.
*   **Potassium Iodide (KI)**: Only for protection against radioactive iodine (nuclear reactor accidents). Not typically useful for blast-driven fallout unless specified.
